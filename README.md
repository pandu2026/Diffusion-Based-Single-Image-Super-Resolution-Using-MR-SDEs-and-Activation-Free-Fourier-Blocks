# Diffusion-Based-Single-Image-Super-Resolution-Using-MR-SDEs-and-Activation-Free-Fourier-Blocks

Image super-resolution is inherently an ill-posed problem, as a single low-resolution (LR) image can correspond to multiple valid high-resolution (HR) images. Super-resolution techniques based on diffusion probabilistic models address this issue by learning the distribution of HR images conditioned on their LR counterparts. Single Image Super-Resolution (SISR) is a fundamental task in computer vision, aimed at generating HR images from LR inputs. While deep learning methods, especially flow-based approaches and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), have greatly enhanced the perceptual quality of images; however, they face challenges such as instability, substantial computational demands, and a restricted diversity in the images produced. In this work, we propose a diffusion probabilistic framework for SISR based on Mean-Reverting Stochastic Differential Equations (MR-SDEs), which explicitly constrain the diffusion trajectory toward the LR-conditioned mean and reduce stochastic variance during sampling. A conditional noise prediction network equipped with an Activation-Free Fourier Block (AFFB) is employed to enhance frequency-aware feature modeling, while a lightweight RRDB-based LR encoder provides stable and informative conditioning. In addition, a dense sampling strategy enables multi-level feature integration, and a color correction module is introduced to mitigate color shifts induced by the diffusion process. Extensive experiments on standard face and general image super-resolution benchmarks show consistent improvements over representative diffusion-based and GAN-based methods in terms of PSNR, SSIM, and perceptual metrics. The results indicate that the proposed design improves reconstruction fidelity and perceptual consistency without introducing additional sampling complexity, demonstrating the effectiveness of mean-reverting diffusion dynamics for super-resolution.
